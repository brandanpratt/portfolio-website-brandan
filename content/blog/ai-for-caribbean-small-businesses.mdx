Every week I talk to business owners in Nassau and across the region who know AI matters but aren't sure where it fits. Most have tried ChatGPT. Few have changed how their business actually runs.

The gap isn't the technology. It's knowing **which problems are worth solving first**. Here's where I consistently see real returns.

## 1. Answering the same questions, all day

Front desks, WhatsApp inboxes and email accounts are full of repeat questions: opening hours, pricing, policies, "did you get my documents?" An assistant grounded in *your* information — not the open internet — can handle the routine questions instantly and hand anything sensitive to a person.

**Start here if:** your team spends hours a day typing the same answers.

## 2. Paperwork in, structured data out

Forms, invoices, applications, IDs, receipts. Much of the region still runs on PDFs and scans. Modern models are very good at pulling structured fields out of messy documents and flagging the ones a human should check.

**Start here if:** someone on your team re-types information from documents into a system.

## 3. Reporting nobody has time to build

Owners often have the data — in a POS, a spreadsheet, an accounting package — but no time to turn it into answers. A small pipeline plus an AI layer that lets you *ask* questions in plain English ("which products slowed down after August?") is now a realistic project for a small business.

## 4. Drafting, not deciding

Proposals, job descriptions, policies, marketing copy, customer replies. AI is an excellent first-draft machine. The win comes from building it into your workflow with your templates and tone, so drafts come out 80% done instead of generic.

## 5. Internal know-how

When one person knows how everything works, the business is fragile. Turning SOPs, manuals and past emails into a searchable internal assistant protects that knowledge and speeds up onboarding.

## How to start without a big budget

1. **Pick one painful, repetitive workflow.** Not "use AI everywhere".
2. **Measure it today.** Hours per week, error rate, response time.
3. **Prototype in days.** A working slice tells you more than a 40-page strategy deck.
4. **Keep a human in the loop** wherever money, health or legal risk is involved.
5. **Scale what works**, kill what doesn't.
