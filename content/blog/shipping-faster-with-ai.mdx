A few years ago, a project like a full client portal with auth, dashboards and integrations meant a small team and a few months. Today I regularly deliver that scope as one engineer, faster — because AI is part of every stage of how I work.

That's not a claim that AI writes the software. It's a claim that **AI removes the slow parts**, so more of my time goes into the parts that matter: understanding the business, designing the system and making it reliable.

## Where AI speeds me up

**Exploring unfamiliar territory.** New API, new framework, new domain? I can get from zero to a working spike in an afternoon instead of days of docs-reading.

**Scaffolding and boilerplate.** Forms, CRUD screens, migrations, typed API clients, test fixtures. Necessary, repetitive and perfect for AI assistance with tools like Cursor and Claude.

**Prototypes clients can touch.** Instead of describing a solution in a document, I can show a clickable version early. Feedback gets sharper and expensive misunderstandings disappear.

**Documentation.** Clear handover docs used to be the first thing cut. Now they're cheap to produce and keep current.

## Where I don't trust it

**Security and access control.** Authentication, permissions and anything touching personal data get written and reviewed by hand, against a checklist.

**Data integrity.** Migrations, financial calculations and reporting logic are tested against real cases. A confident-looking query that's subtly wrong is worse than no query.

**Architecture decisions.** AI is a useful sparring partner, but it doesn't carry the context of your business, your budget or your team's ability to maintain the result. That judgment is the job.

**Anything I can't explain.** If I can't explain why a piece of code works, it doesn't ship.

## What this means for clients

- **Shorter timelines** for the same scope.
- **Lower cost** for prototypes and MVPs.
- **More of the budget** on the hard problems, not the boilerplate.
- **Production standards** unchanged — the same discipline I apply to government digital services.
